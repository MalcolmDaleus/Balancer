# What is Balancer?

Balancer is a budgeting web app that allows users to take an active and honest role in their spending habits. The idea was born from a need I saw in my own daily life and the lives of those around me: In a world where spending money is as simple as tapping a card to a reader, it becomes increasingly hard to properly manage one's own finances and properly know the answer to "What did I spend all that money on this month?". The goal was an app that was simple to use and gave instant feedback to promote budgeting habits from the first interaction. 

## The technology involved
The tech stack is Laravel 12 for its extensive backend system made for intricate logic with a React frontend for a clean and appealing look with support from TailwindCSS and ShadCN components. 

## What does it do?
The root of the app is based in taking in numbers from 5 main budgeting domains: Income, Debt, Spending, Recurring Spending, and Savings Contributions. The app uses a monthly cycle, taking in data provided by the user and displaying it in a balance sheet to see clearly how things add up. The data from these domains is used for several types of calculations that provide the user with real time analysis of their spending habits, and generates a historical report to see their progress over time, with the end goal of saving money. The idea is for the actual data input to be so simple that it becomes a seamless part of the user's daily life, while providing real value to them through pure analysis. When a user taps their card for a coffee, writing it down is as simple as opening up the app, typing in "Coffee" and "$3.45" and allowing the app to incorporate it into the final analysis. The audience is a user conscious of their need for budgeting, but intimidated by keeping track of numbers manually, and committing to a complicated app that makes basic tracking a nightmare. It's simple, but incredibly useful.

## The different pieces
Balancer has several different zones or "modules" that control the functionality and data presentation. 

### Balance Sheet
The first is the Balance Sheet module, which lists the current running monthly totals for each domain with detailed breakdowns when available and lists the monthly net cashflow as "rollover". This is where you see the simple analysis of how the numbers are adding up each month.

### Ledger
The ledger is the control center for all financial data. This module creates transactions for each type of domain and lists them with various editing options depending on the domain. The transactions themselves are either directly created on demand by the user, or they can be set to be generated regularly on a schedule.
#### Feed
This is the global list for every transaction made by the user, with the title, date and domain attached.  
#### Income
All positive cashflow broken down by type: Regular, Irregular, or Refunds. Regular income can be set on schedule which will create an income transaction at the frequency set. Irregular income allows for one off or unorthodox income frequencies and creates a new transaction directly. Refunds are automatically created when a purchase is marked as refunded in its own domain tab and are never directly generated by the user. The transactions themselves can be edited or deleted whether they were automatically generated or created by the user. The regular income streams can be edited as well, with all changes applying to the **next** iteration of its cycle.  
#### Purchases
This domain records day to day spending, whether its a life changing purchase or the occacional cup of coffee. The user can (and is encouraged) to create categories for different types of spending, which helps with statistical breakdowns and records in the balance sheets. Any purchase can be refunded, paartially or fully, which will mark it as refunded in the feed, and create a corresponding "Refund" income transaction with the amount refunded.  
#### Debts
Personal debts and other long term financial obligations are covered by this domain. Each debt is given a balance and can be paid off using transactions for each payment. This way, the debt itself is acknowledged, as well as the time and amount of each individual payment whenever they are made. Once full payment of the debt is made its marked automatically as settled. Debts can also be forgiven early, ending the obligation without full repayment.  
#### Recurring Purchases
The recurring purchaase domain is functionally similar to the income domain, but with negative cashflow. This covers any regular spending such as bills, subscriptions, or even something known to be a regular spending habit. Streams can be created for specific regular charges that will generate their own transactions at the frequency set, with any edits applying to **future** charges from the edit date. The transactions themselves can be edited or deleted whether they were automatically generated or created by the user.  
#### Savings
The most simple domain which simply records manual cashflow into savings. It's meant to be an intentional action the user takes when a certain amount of money is set aside any month or widthdrawn from savings. 

### Budget
The budget module houses the user's financial goals and keeps track of how much the user has left to spend according to the budget for that month. The module includes a global monthly spending limit and and spending limits according to the user's custom purchase domain categories. It also lists whether all recurring spending obligations (bills, subscriptions) have been met for that month and plans for paying off debts and putting money into savings. The limits never interfere with the core functionality; users can still record purchases that go beyond the monthly limit, and the state of the budget will record that the limit has been surpased.

### Past Balance Sheets
Users can see basic breakdowns of the five domains in past months in this module. It acts as a historial module for monthly cashflow.

### Statistics
This module gives more life to the numbers and records. Data from all five domains is compiled and used to create monthly and historical graphs and charts for a more visual way of viewing the financial records. The module includes line charts for historical trends month to month, bar charts for type and categorical comparisons, pie charts to see how different portions of a financial domain contribute to the whole picture, and big picture markers for contextual analysis. 

## The golden rule - Record fidelity
The baseline values of this application are honesty, and data fidelity. The user's job is honesty. The goal is for users to faithfully record their spending habits and cashflow. This allows for maximum benefit of being able to have all the information available in a clear way so that they can make their own changes to spending. Our job is to faithfully store and keep these records, and be able to produce them on demand.  
One of the most important aspects of the app is the month locking feature. The app operates on a monthly cycle for record collection, then closes future data mutation when the month ends to perserve historical data. The implications of this monthly closure are different depending on the domain and instrument used in said domain.
### Transactions
Within a month, before the balance sheet is closed, transactions can be created, altered, and deleted with no limit. Once the month closes, modification is closed completely so that historical data isn't constantly changing and complicating calculations. Certain domains expose secondary actions on their transactions like refunding purchases or forgiving debts, which do not directly modify calculable data in past months and can always be accessed outside of the origin month. 
### Schedulers aand Transaction Generators
Schedulers and transaction generators have even stricter guidelines on top of month locking to prevent widespread transactional erasure or data slippage. While a transaction exists that is tied to a generator, it cannot be deleted and can only be archived, which hides it from the normal tab view. This preserves the integrity of the interactions between schedulers and records and prevents records from being orphaned if a user decides to delete a deactivated schedule. Transaction generators in general always have a simple way of being deactivated to stop future scheduled generation and can be visually hidden for organization purposes, but hard deletion only can happen for generators with no associated transactions and is impossible for ones that have transactions in closed months
### Categories
Categories are a particular case where they are very heavily used in the presentation side of the application but play a relatively minor role in the logic of the transactions. Any category within a domain that is attached to an existing transaction cannot be deleted directly and only archived to be hidden from view. This preserves the historical presentation of certain statistics series.
